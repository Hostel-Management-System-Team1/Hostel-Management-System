# Deviations from the SRS, SAD and Test Plan

Every place the implementation differs from the submitted documents is recorded
here, with the reason. These are explained during the final demo.

| # | Document & section | What the document says | What we built instead | Reason | Sprint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 1 | SRS 2.5 and 3.3, SAD 3.6 — student identity from the institutional ERP | A read-only HTTPS/JSON lookup against the ERP checks the enrolment number | A stub adapter (`apps/admissions/erp.py`) that only checks the enrolment number's format; the applicant types their own details. A real client plugs in through `HMS_ERP_ADAPTER` | No ERP sandbox exists for the course project | 1 |
| 2 | SAD 3.4 — room-inventory master belongs to the Reporting & Admin Service | Room, block and bed masters (HMS-F-061) sit in Reporting & Admin | They live in the Admission & Allocation app (`apps/admissions`) next to the allocation that uses them; the administrator maintains them in the admin site | Allocation and the inventory change in one transaction; a separate app would only add cross-app imports | 1 |
| 3 | SRS 2.5 and 3.3, SAD 3.6 — payments through a PCI-DSS compliant gateway | A real provider's hosted page, with signed callbacks and refunds | A bundled mock gateway (`apps/fees/gateway.py`): a stand-in hosted page that asks for no card or bank details and sends the same HMAC-signed callback a provider would. The HMS stores only the method name and the gateway reference. Refunds are recorded in the vacating workflow, not sent to a provider | No merchant account or provider sandbox for a course project | 1 |
| 4 | SAD 3.6 — retry with exponential backoff and a dead-letter queue for gateway calls | Gateway and notification failures are retried with backoff | A gateway outage is reported to the resident at once (API 502) and the invoice stays Unpaid; the resident retries. Notification retries are built in Sprint 2 | The mock gateway cannot fail on its own, so retry logic for it would be untestable | 1 |
| 5 | SRS 4.1 HMS-F-003 — transfer to another bed | The warden reallocates a resident; source vacant and target occupied in one transaction | Implemented as specified. Moving to a room of a different type does not re-issue the fee invoice raised at allocation; the warden is told so on the transfer form | The SRS does not say how fees change after a transfer; re-invoicing would need an accounts rule the team has not defined | 1 |
| 6 | SRS 5.1.2 HMS-SR-001 — TLS 1.2 or higher on all connections | The system enforces TLS 1.2+ for browser, gateway and notification traffic | Django redirects plain HTTP to HTTPS, sends HSTS and sets secure cookies outside development; the TLS 1.2 minimum and cipher list are set in the reverse proxy (`deploy/nginx.conf.example`). Outbound gateway and notification calls are covered once a real provider replaces the mocks (rows 3 and 4) | TLS terminates at the web server, not in Django; the scan in TC-SEC-01 needs a deployed server, so the app cannot prove it on its own | 1 |
| 7 | SRS 3.3 — SMS or email notification service | Templated SMS or email to residents, guardians and staff | Email only, through Django's mail backend (console in development, SMTP through the `EMAIL_*` settings). No SMS provider | No SMS gateway account for a course project; email covers every notification the SRS lists | 2 |
| 8 | SRS 4.3 HMS-F-020, SRS 3.2 — RFID or biometric reader through the vendor SDK | Each scan at the reader creates an IN or OUT record | Readers post events to `POST /api/v1/gate/movements/device` with a shared key (`X-Gate-Device-Key`); no vendor SDK is integrated. The guard's **Gate log** page is the manual fallback | No reader hardware or SDK is available; any reader that can make an HTTPS request can be connected | 2 |
| 9 | SRS 4.3 HMS-F-022 — alert pushed within 5 minutes of the threshold | A push alert to the warden | `process_curfew_alerts`, scheduled every 5 minutes, emails the wardens and the alert shows on the warden's dashboard and gate log. No browser or mobile push | Email plus the dashboard reaches the warden without a push service; the 5-minute bound depends on the schedule | 2 |
| 10 | SRS 4.4 HMS-F-030, F-031 — weekly versioned menu; opt-out for a date range; bill = rate × consumed days | One menu version per week; a resident skips a range of days | Each meal is published on its own date; a resident skips individual meals (24 hours ahead). The bill is ₹50 for every published meal in the month that was not skipped | Meal-level opt-outs are finer than days and a range is several meals; pricing per meal matches how the mess counts food | 2 |
| 11 | SRS 4.5 HMS-F-042 — SLA values are configurable | The 48- and 96-hour SLAs can be changed | The SLAs are fixed in code (`Complaint.sla_deadline`); escalation emails every Hostel Administrator and writes an audit entry | The SRS values were used as given; a settings screen was not needed for the demo | 2 |
| 12 | SRS 4.6 HMS-F-050 — visitor's relationship to the resident; identity-proof number stored encrypted | A relationship field; the ID number encrypted at rest | The relationship goes in the purpose of visit; the ID number is stored as entered and is shown only to the guard, warden and administrator | Field-level encryption needs key management that the project does not have; access control limits who can read it | 2 |
| 13 | SRS 4.7 HMS-F-060 — reports for a chosen period; occupancy per block and floor | A period picker; occupancy totals per block and floor, including blocked beds | Reports show the current state. The occupancy export lists every room with its block, floor, status and occupied beds; the page shows overall totals | Current figures were enough for the warden's use; the exported rows can be grouped by block and floor in Excel | 2 |
| 14 | SRS 5 HMS-NF-001, NF-002, NF-003 — measured in a production-like environment | 200 concurrent users on 2,000 records; 99.5% monthly uptime from operations monitoring; a quarterly restore drill | The load test (Locust, 2,000 seeded residents, p95 1.6 s, no failures) ran on a developer laptop with Waitress, with the database and load generator on one machine; see `docs/performance/`. Uptime is checked by a scheduled script on the same machine, and the nightly backup is scheduled there; no restore drill has been timed | No production-like server is available to the team; the tools are in the repository so the measurements can be repeated on one | 2 |
| 15 | Test Plan 2 and 10, SAD 3.4 and 3.6, SRS 3.4 — gate terminal client with a local IndexedDB buffer and ordered replay | A browser client at the gate keeps entries while the network is down and replays them in order | Not built. The gate API accepts an event's original time (`occurred_at`) and a unique `external_event_id`, so a reader or client that buffers events can replay them later: they keep their original time and a repeated event is ignored (`test_buffered_event_preserves_original_timestamp`, `test_duplicate_device_event_is_not_recorded_twice`). The guard's **Gate log** page needs the network | No gate hardware to buffer for, and the server-side half is what a replay depends on; a browser buffer would have nothing real to test against | 2 |
| 16 | Test Plan 6 — test tools | Jira for defects, JMeter for load, Postman and Selenium for API and UI tests | Defects are listed in `docs/qa/TEST_SUMMARY.md` and the GitHub board; load testing used Locust; API and page tests are Django/DRF test-client tests that run in GitHub Actions on every PR; pages were checked by hand in Chrome. CI uses Python 3.12 as planned (developer machines also ran 3.13) | The team already worked in GitHub, and test-client tests run in CI without a browser or a running server | 2 |
| 17 | Test Plan 5.2, 5.5 and 15 (TC-UX-01, TC-SEC-01, TC-SEC-06) — usability trial, TLS scan, ZAP scan, four browsers | A 10-user allocation trial, a live TLS scan, an OWASP ZAP report, and Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari | An axe-core WCAG 2.1 AA audit of 33 pages (0 violations), checked at 360 px, in Chrome only. Injection and stored-markup tests are written by hand (`hms/test_security.py`). No user trial, TLS scan or ZAP run. These three cases are PARTIAL in the System Validation Report | The trial needs ten untrained wardens, the scan needs a deployed server, and no ZAP install was set up in time; the hand-written tests cover the same attack classes for every page | 2 |
| 18 | Test Plan 7 — test schedule | Testing between 20 Sep and 6 Oct 2026 | Testing was done after the build, 2 to 8 Nov 2026 (Part-3 window), in two passes: the QA register on 2 Nov and the full reports on 8 Nov | The build finished at the end of Sprint 2, so the plan's dates were not met; the reports are in `docs/reports/` | 2 |
